MULTIPLY

Multiplication Moves

Momo here! Multiplication is just fast adding — the same number, over and over. Let's build it, group it, and flip it around.

📖 How it works (tap to open the lessons)

Equal groups

3 groups of 5 apples is 3 × 5. Repeated addition: 5 + 5 + 5 = 15.

3 × 5 = 15, the same as 5 + 5 + 5.

Arrays

Rows and columns of objects. Rows × columns = total.

4 rows of 6 = 4 × 6 = 24. Turn it sideways: 6 rows of 4 = 6 × 4 = 24, too!

Multiplication words

  • factor × factor = product
  • 4 × 7 = 28 → 4 and 7 are factors, 28 is the product

Skip counting

Counting by the same number again and again lands you on that times table. Pick a number to try!

Three special rules

  • Flip-it rule: a × b = b × a (order doesn't matter)
  • Times-1 rule: any number × 1 = that same number
  • Zero rule: any number × 0 = 0
9 × 1 = 9  ·  9 × 0 = 0  ·  6 × 8 = 8 × 6

Multiples build up

Word problem clues

Multiply when you see: each, every, in all, altogether, or "groups of".

6 tables, 4 chairs at each table. How many chairs in all? 6 × 4 = 24.

DIVIDE

Division & Fact Families

Kumo says: division is the un-doer of multiplication. Share it out evenly, or subtract the same amount again and again.

📖 How it works (tap to open the lessons)

Equal sharing

12 stickers shared equally among 3 friends. How many does each friend get?

12 ÷ 3 = 4. Each friend gets 4 stickers.

Equal grouping

12 stickers, 4 in every group. How many groups can you make?

12 ÷ 4 = 3 groups. Same numbers, different question!

Repeated subtraction

Start at 15. Subtract 5 again and again until you hit 0. Count the subtractions.

15 − 5 = 10, 10 − 5 = 5, 5 − 5 = 0. That's 3 subtractions, so 15 ÷ 5 = 3.

Division words

  • dividend ÷ divisor = quotient
  • 20 ÷ 4 = 5 → 20 is the dividend, 4 the divisor, 5 the quotient

Fact families

3, 6, and 18 make one family of 4 related facts:

3 × 6 = 18  ·  6 × 3 = 18  ·  18 ÷ 3 = 6  ·  18 ÷ 6 = 3

Zero and one in division

  • n ÷ 1 = n (7 ÷ 1 = 7)
  • n ÷ n = 1, if n isn't 0 (7 ÷ 7 = 1)
  • 0 ÷ n = 0 (0 ÷ 7 = 0)
  • You can never divide by 0.

    Parent Corner

    How to use this page
    • Start with ⚡ Sprint (1 minute). It times every answer and colors the Fact Map: green = fast and right twice in a row, yellow = right but slow (still counting), pink = missed.
    • Sprints pick her pink and yellow facts more often, so they turn green over time. "Flip it" mode pairs each × fact with its ÷ fact.
    • Start each session in the Array Builder or Skip-Count Hopper for 3–5 minutes, then do one Practice Pod round.
    • Practice Pods mix everyday and trickier questions automatically; there is no separate harder level to pick.
    • The Belt Dojo is meant to be revisited daily — a couple of belts a week is plenty.
    • Every question has a "Show me how" explanation, so the answer key is built in.
    • A movement break pops up every so often. Progress saves in this browser only.
    Backup or move progress (phone ↔ tablet)

    Progress lives only in this browser. To move it: tap Make backup code, copy the code, send it to the other device, paste it there and tap Restore.

    What she should be able to do (FCPS Units 3, 6, 9 & 12)
    • Represent multiplication as equal groups, arrays, and repeated addition, and division as equal sharing, equal grouping, and repeated subtraction.
    • Recall multiplication and division facts through 12 × 12 fluently.
    • Understand and use fact families to relate a multiplication fact to its two matching division facts.
    • Apply the commutative property, the identity property (× 1), and the zero property (× 0) informally, without needing the formal names.
    • Solve one- and two-step word problems involving multiplication and division, including "how many in all," "how many each," and "how many groups" situations.
    Watch for these common slips
    • Mixing up the dividend and divisor (writing 3 ÷ 12 instead of 12 ÷ 3).
    • Forgetting that a × b and b × a always give the same product.
    • Skip-counting errors — losing track partway through a longer times table.
    • Thinking any number divided by 0 is 0, instead of understanding it can't be done.
    • Confusing "3 groups of 5" with "5 groups of 3" when writing the matching equation for a word problem.
